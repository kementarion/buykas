# 5. The Kaspa Emission Curve: Debates and Research


On November 7, 2021, the Kaspa mainnet launched.

For the first two weeks, the block reward was random, taking a value between 1 and 1000 coins.

The design intent was to make earnings unpredictable and reduce the interest of professional miners.

::: warning A bug in the code……
The mean of the random distribution was mistakenly written as 750 instead of 500—this bug was only discovered years later, at the cost of about 300 million extra coins issued.
:::


For the following six months, the reward was fixed at 500 coins per second.


The real monetary policy began on May 8, 2022: the reward started at 440 coins per second and was multiplied by 0.9439 each month, halving exactly every twelve months.

::: info Further Explanation

- The subsidy is defined **per second**, not **per block**. So when the block rate was raised from 1 block per second to 10 blocks per second in May 2025 (the Crescendo upgrade), the reward per block was divided by ten. **The number of coins issued per second is unchanged, and a per-second block-rate upgrade does not affect the money supply.**

- Under this algorithm, at any point in time, the coming year will mine **half** of all remaining supply.


:::

## (1). Why Emit So Fast?

The reason given by the official documentation can be summed up in one sentence:

> **ASICs are ultimately inevitable, so get most of the circulating supply out before they appear.** 

::: info

Original text (official publication channel, January 2023):

> "The short emission schedule and high deflation rate were designed to address the ASIC dominance problem. Since ASICs on the network are ultimately inevitable, by the time ASICs appear, most of the circulating supply will already have been issued. In addition, the high block rate, together with high TPS, lowers the barrier to hardware entry (for both pools and solo miners). If ASICs do eventually appear, the network share required to see a return is much lower than Bitcoin's. If Kaspa reaches Bitcoin's scale, mining with just a few ASICs per month will still produce blocks—this privilege will not be reserved only for large farms."

:::

The goal was to issue 80% of the supply before ASICs entered the retail market. In practice it was 65% (about 70% at the time of mass shipments).


| Time | Event | Cumulative Mined |
|---|---|---|
| 2023-03 | IceRiver announces KS1 (1 TH/s) and KS2 (2 TH/s)—the first mass-produced Kaspa miners | ~60% |
| Summer 2023 | First batch of KS3 (8 TH/s) ships | 65.0% (June) |
| 2023-08 | Bitmain KS3 (8.3–9.4 TH/s) goes on sale | 70.2% |
| 2023-09 | Desktop miner KS0 goes on sale, miners enter retail | Past 70% |



**Why mine and emit quickly before ASICs appear?**


## (2). What Is an ASIC? Why Resist ASICs?

An ASIC is an "Application-Specific Integrated Circuit," designed and fabricated **for one specific algorithm**. It cannot mine other coins, play games, or do rendering.

**Precisely because it does only one thing, its efficiency is absurdly high.**

::: info An Example


- A consumer-grade graphics card, the RTX 4090, does about 2.0 GH/s on kHeavyHash (WhatToMine lists 2.00 GH/s @ 240 W)

- The Bitmain KS7 shipped in May 2025: 40 TH/s, 3,080 W power draw, 77 J/TH efficiency, priced at about $1,500–1,900


> **The hash rate of one miner equals twenty thousand graphics cards.**

And its price is two thousand dollars, with a power draw of three kilowatts.

Twenty thousand graphics cards would require over thirty million dollars and a combined power draw of over six thousand kilowatts.


:::



For the network, this is a good thing: the same money buys far more security. Bitcoin's total network hash rate today is 930 EH/s, the vast majority of it from ASICs.

**But security and decentralization are two different things.**

In the GPU era, a single home machine could enter, and participants were tens of thousands of dispersed retail miners.

In the ASIC era, the barrier becomes "able to prepay hundreds of thousands and handle logistics and power contracts," and participants are a few dozen industrial mining farms.

This change is not right or wrong in itself, but it happened to occur **in the early period when the network most needed decentralization**, so the cost is different.

::: info Kaspa's Hash Rate Trajectory


- September 2022: 0.2 PH/s
- June 2023: 1.1 PH/s, the vast majority of participants on GPUs
- December 2023: 110.5 PH/s—up about 96x in half a year
- August 2024: 789.7 PH/s
- January 2025: 1,378.9 PH/s (single-point peak of 1,568.6 PH/s on January 31)
- September 2026: 327.8 PH/s

:::

A hash rate surge does not mean more people—quite the opposite: the same hash rate has concentrated into fewer hands.

ASICs are custom products, so the companies that make them know when the chips tape out, when they enter mass production, and when they can be powered on. Before the miners go on public sale, they can absolutely mine with their own machines for a while first—this has long precedent in the mining industry, and today several major manufacturers are themselves among the largest miners.

How much is this head start worth? The most intuitive way to calculate it is by "daily output per unit of hash rate": in June 2023, each TH/s could mine about 15,400 KAS per day; by December, the same hash rate yielded only about 113 coins. A difference of about 136x. The gap in between is the price of "starting to mine half a year early"—by the time retail buyers get their miners, the difficulty has already been pushed up.


::: warning Consequences

- GPU miners have to pay electricity bills and hardware loans; they cannot hold onto coins, which continuously flow to the market;

- Coins held by industrial farms and manufacturers are more likely to sit on the balance sheet, **becoming a long-term overhang pressing down on the circulating float.**

- **Who gets the coins first determines the market structure for many years to come.**

:::


## (3). Insider Advantage Is Also a Kind of Unfairness!

In the first six months after launch (that is, the initial deflationary period), about 27% of the total supply was issued; by the twelfth month after launch (November 2022) the cumulative total exceeded 50%, and by the thirteenth to fourteenth months it exceeded 65%.

In other words, the distribution window was only one year. Very few people could stand inside this window.

And the unfairness can be broken down into three layers, each with a different cause:

- **Information layer**: knowing about this chain, understanding its technology, and being willing to buy cards, build rigs, and join pools when no one is paying attention — the barrier here is cognition.

- **Capital layer**: rewards during the window were extremely high, meaning "the same card could mine dozens of times more coins," so those who could stake more capital had their share amplified.

- **Time layer**: the window was only one year. Enter half a year late, and the output per unit of hash rate drops by an order of magnitude (see the 136x in the previous section). This gap cannot be made up by effort, only by entering earlier.


::: info

A typical example is that after DAGLabs' investors stopped funding in mid-2021 and the project was handed over to the community, it used the remainder of the initial investment to rent cloud hash power and mine for about five months until the money ran out—the coins mined were no more than 3% of the total, most likely close to 2.5%, and were distributed to investors and former employees. At today's price ($0.0322), this batch is worth $23 million to $28 million; at the July 2024 peak price of $0.20741, it was worth $149 million to $179 million.

**The mining process was fair, because a large number of other ordinary miners were mining alongside them at the same time.**

:::

"Early people took too much" is not an illusion; it holds up in the data. There is no absolute fairness in this world.






## (4). What If the Emission Speed Had Been Slowed Down?

- **Slower means today's security budget is indeed larger.** 

    * If it had been changed to "halve every two years," today's subsidy would be 2.3x the current level; "halve every three years" would be 2.6x.


- **The cost of slowing down falls entirely on distribution.** 

    * The proportion already issued when ASICs arrived, and the share industrial farms could take: from one-third to one-half, or even close to sixty percent.


- **Inflation is the hard flaw of the slow option.** 
    
    * Annual inflation would rise from 1.8% to 5.6%–8.3%, which is 3 to 4.5 times the current level.

    * For a chain that wants to do payments and settlement, this is a positioning problem—no merchant wants to accept a unit of account that shrinks by five to eight percent every year.



If the two-year halving had been chosen back then, the starting point would have been only 230 coins/second (the actual was 440), and the coins issued in the first year would have been about half as many—in 2022, when GPU miners were still on the sidelines, this was not a small bet.

This is a choice between **"how strong the start is"** and **"how long it can sustain."**

This is not a question of "aggressive versus prudent," but of which problem you trade for which.

::: warning Views and Reflections

- Fast emission solved the distribution problem of 2022, at the cost of moving the security problem from 2140 up to 2029.

- Slow emission would push the security problem into the 2030s, at the cost of **handing distribution to ASIC manufacturers**, **dragging out inflation**, and very likely failing to buy a community that survives to today.

:::


::: info Why the Early Period Needs Enough "Selling Pressure + Circulation"


In projects with a pure PoW fair launch (no pre-mine, no team allocation), early coins come almost entirely from miners.

Miners fall into two categories:

- GPU/FPGA miners: high electricity and hardware depreciation costs (opex-heavy). They must continuously sell most of the coins they mine to cover electricity and opportunity costs, or they simply run at a loss.

- ASIC/institutional miners: dominated by capital expenditure (capex), with relatively low operating costs, and more inclined to hoard coins or be more patient.

**If emission is too slow (or early rewards are not high enough):**

- Early circulating supply is very small. It is hard for ordinary people to buy a meaningful amount of coins at a reasonable cost, and the holder base cannot grow.

- The market lacks natural sell-side liquidity. Bid-ask spreads are wide, depth is poor, speculator interest is low, and price discovery is difficult.

- The community cannot quickly form a holder group with "skin in the game." What developers, content creators, and exchange listing teams see is "a dead chain + low trading volume," and the project easily falls into a death spiral of "no users → no developers → even fewer users."

- Hash rate growth depends on genuine interest and profit, not purely on rewards. If the early community is weak, it is very hard to catch up later.

**Conversely, moderately fast emission + a GPU-dominated period brings:**

- A large number of coins forced onto the market (the natural behavior of GPU miners).

- Coins widely dispersed into more hands, forming an initial holder pool.

- Improved liquidity → attracts more buyers and speculators → price volatility and attention → more people join mining/holding/discussion → positive feedback.

- By the time ASICs arrive, most of the supply is already dispersed, rather than concentrated in the hands of a few hardware manufacturers.

This is the "sweet spot" that Kaspa's designers repeatedly emphasize: too fast lets the very earliest people take too much; too slow hands distribution to the later ASIC-dominated players, and the community may not survive until then.

Long-term high inflation is the other side of the problem: even if there is early circulation, a continuous flood of new coins keeps diluting holders, suppressing the price and driving miners and users away, forming a "death spiral."

:::

An early community is like a startup that needs "seed users and cash flow": the natural selling pressure of miners pushes coins to the market, creating tradable circulation and a holder base; without this process, the project lacks people with "skin in the game" to spread, develop, and use it. Slow emission delays distribution and may buy a theoretically fairer long-term structure, but many projects have already become deserted before reaching that "long term."

**This is not an absolute rule (some slow-emission projects survive on narrative or technology), but it recurs in historical data: the cold-start failure rate is significantly higher under the combination of "insufficient early circulation + continuous dilution later." Kaspa's fast emission is precisely an active choice aimed at this pain point.**






## (5). What to Do Going Forward?


First, the numbers (price converted at $0.0322):

| Time | Daily Output | USD/Day |
|---|---|---|
| September 2026 | 1.886 million coins | about $61,000 |
| September 2030 | 118,000 coins | about $3,800 |
| September 2034 | 7,367 coins | about $240 |



**The available paths, each with its cost:**

- **Higher price.** The security budget follows the price.

- **A fee market that actually grows.** This requires real settlement demand (payments, stablecoins, L1 contracts). Toccata is paving the way in this direction, and the observable metric is fees as a share of total miner revenue.

- **Tail emission.** Keeping a permanent subsidy like Monero or Grin. The technical barrier is not high—Kaspa has already successfully executed two major hard forks (Crescendo in May 2025 and Toccata on June 30, 2026). The real barrier is social consensus: it would change the rule of "a total supply of 28.7 billion, reaching zero around 2057," publicly promised from day one, and would require miners, exchanges, and holders to agree at the same time—yet Kaspa happens to lack the ability to organize such a vote. It has no foundation, no governance token, and no institution that can represent the whole network. The official WIKI lists three paths side by side—"raise the standard fee," "add tail emission," and "charge a demurrage fee on long-dormant UTXOs"—and then states that none of them have entered discussion for actual implementation. There is currently no plan for any tail issuance.
